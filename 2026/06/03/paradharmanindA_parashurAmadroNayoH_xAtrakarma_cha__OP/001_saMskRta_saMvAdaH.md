\# Translation: The Condemnation of Pardharmaniinda (Censure of Practicing Another's Dharma) - The Cases of Parasurama and Drona  
  
\## The Varna-Ashrama System and Prescribed Duties  
  
In the varna-ashrama system, the duties (sva-dharma) prescribed for Brahmins are tranquility, self-control, and similar virtues, while for Kshatriyas, warfare, valor, and similar qualities are prescribed as their duties. The scriptural principle states: "Death in one's own dharma is auspicious; the dharma of another brings fear." When a Brahmin, abandoning his own dharma, practices the dharma of Kshatriyas, the great sages have censured such a person as one engaged in pardharmaniinda (practicing another's dharma) in the scriptures.  
  
\## The Case of Parasurama  
  
The great sage Parasurama, though a Brahmin by birth, adopted the dharma of Kshatriyas through his actions. Therefore, in both the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, his practice of another's dharma is censured in many places. In the Bala Kanda of Valmiki's Ramayana, when an angry Parasurama blocked Rama's path, the frightened King Dasharatha pleaded with him to become peaceful. There, Dasharatha explicitly condemned the Bhargava's (Parasurama's) practice of another's dharma:  
  
**"Being pacified from the rage of Kshatriyas, you are a Brahmin of great austerity. You should grant safety to my sons, who are mere children. Born in the family of Bhargavas who are devoted to Vedic study and vows, you took up weapons, O thousand-eyed one (Indra), breaking your pledge."**  
  
\## Bhishma's Criticism in the Mahabharata  
  
Similarly, in the Udyoga Parva of the Mahabharata, when Parasurama fights with Bhishma, even Bhishma censures his practice of another's dharma. Bhishma states that if a Brahmin fights like a Kshatriya, killing him in warfare is not brahmahatya (killing a Brahmin):  
  
**"Whoever slays an armed Brahmin fighting in battle like a Kshatriya, that act does not constitute brahmahatya (the sin of killing a Brahmin)."**  
  
Bhishma further proclaims that even a guru must be abandoned if he abandons the path of dharma:  
  
**"Even a guru who has become polluted and does not know what should and should not be done, who has gone astray—he should be abandoned."**  
  
Bhishma tells Parasurama: "I stand firm in Kshatriya dharma; you have fallen from Brahminhood."  
  
**"I am indeed a Kshatriya, O Rama, established in my Kshatriya dharma."**  
  
\## Parasurama's Father's Intervention  
  
In that very great battle, Parasurama's ancestors appeared in the sky and turned him away from practicing another's dharma. They explicitly instructed him that warfare is the dharma of Kshatriyas, not of Brahmins. They said that a Brahmin's true wealth is Vedic study and austerity:  
  
**"Do not, dear child, commit such an audacious act again in any way. Do not go to battle with Bhishma, especially since he is a Kshatriya. This is the dharma of a Kshatriya, O descendant of Bhrigu—that he engages in warfare. Vedic study and observance of vows—these are the supreme wealth of Brahmins."**  
  
\## The Case of Drona  
  
Furthermore, in the Kurukshetra war, when the preceptor Drona abandoned the dharma appropriate to Brahmins and engaged in terrible warfare with Kshatriya dharma, even the great sages like Vasishtha told him that practicing another's dharma was not proper for him:  
  
**"For one learned in the Vedas and their auxiliaries, devoted to truth and dharma—especially for a Brahmin—this is not proper for you."**  
  
\## Conclusion  
  
Therefore, from these examples it is clear that in ancient times, the observance of varna-dharma (caste duty) according to birth was of utmost importance. There was a rule that Brahmins should not take up weapons. Whenever this boundary was transgressed by Parasurama and others, or by Drona and his ilk, their practice of another's dharma was invariably censured.
